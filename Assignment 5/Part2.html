Cracks Beneath the Surface

The weeks that followed turned the lake into their sanctuary. Lina and Dave found themselves there almost every evening, sharing pieces of their worlds and finding solace in each other’s company. One such evening, as the sun began to sink into the horizon, Lina stared out at the water, her hands fidgeting with the edges of her sweater. “Do you ever get tired?” she asked softly. Dave, who was balancing a stick on his finger, tilted his head. “Of what?”. “Of pretending everything’s fine.” Lina’s voice wavered. She turned to him, her eyes glistening with unshed tears. “It’s… it’s exhausting, Dave. The chemo, the way my parents hover over me, treating me like I’ll break. I know it’s hard for them, too. But sometimes, I just wish I could…” “Live like me?” Dave finished, his voice uncharacteristically gentle. Lina nodded, her tears spilling over as she buried her face in her hands. “It’s not fair. I didn’t ask for this.” Her words dissolved into sobs as she leaned into him. Dave froze, caught off guard by the rawness of her emotion. Slowly, awkwardly, he wrapped his arms around her, offering silent comfort. When her tears subsided, Lina sniffled and pulled back, her cheeks flushed with embarrassment. “Sorry,” she mumbled. “Don’t be,” Dave said, rubbing the back of his neck. “Uh… do you wanna… I mean, should I tell you my story?” Lina nodded, eager to shift the focus off herself.

Dave’s grin faded, replaced by a somber expression. “It’s not much of a story, but… when I was fifteen, my mom died in a car crash. A truck hit her because she forgot to use her indicator.” His voice was steady, but his hands trembled slightly. “After that, my dad changed. He used to be fun, you know? Like me. But now he’s… strict, always on edge. I think he blames himself.” Lina’s heart ached for him. “I’m so sorry, Dave.” “It’s fine,” he said quickly, forcing a smile. “It’s why I’m like this, though. Carefree, reckless. It’s my way of keeping the best part of my dad alive.” He paused, then grinned. “Anyway, enough of the heavy stuff. You ever thought about skating?” Lina blinked, startled by the sudden shift. “Skating? Me?” “Yeah! The wind on your face, the rush of freedom… it’s the best feeling in the world.” Dave jumped to his feet, extending a hand to her. “Come on, I’ll teach you.” Lina hesitated, shaking her head. “I’ll fall.” Dave’s grin widened. “Then I’ll catch you.” She laughed nervously but refused. “Not today.” Dave shrugged, letting it go. “Fair enough. But one day, you’ll feel that freedom, I promise.”

Lina stood up wiping the ground and dead grass from her skirt. “it’s getting late, I should probably head home” She said holding her books and walking away. “Alright Lini” Dave said, “Lini?” Lina turned back looking puzzled “Oh yeah, Lina is such a unique name I don’t think I would meet someone with the name, so I need to use it to the fullest with you and call you every pet name I can make up from lina” he said as he chuckled. “You really never get tired” Lina replied as she let out a sigh of contempt. But before she could turn and head home, she asked him “so where are you heading to now, free boy”, he let out a laugh and said “Not home, my friend is having a party at his place, wanna come” waving his hands, he gestured to her. Lina thought for a moment and told him goodbye and finally started walking back home.

Summary

Dave and Lina grow closer, spending time by the lake and opening up about their struggles. Lina shares the emotional weight of her battle with cancer and her frustrations with her parents' protectiveness. In turn, Dave reveals the pain of losing his mother and how it shaped his carefree persona. Their bond deepens as they comfort each other, finding strength in their shared vulnerabilities.
